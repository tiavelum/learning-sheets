GitLab, pictured as a restaurant

Learning sheet · page 1 of 2

This restaurant is a picture of GitLab: one restaurant stands for one project, the place where a team plans, writes, checks and serves its software. Follow one change along the dashed line. Page two gives the facts behind each numbered item.

Office · plan and write

Issue A ticket on the office board names one wish or problem, such as a guest asking for a vegan starter, until someone deals with it.

Repository The house recipe book holds every recipe and keeps each earlier version of every page, with who changed it and when.

Branch A cook tries one change on a separate set of draft pages in the book, while the house pages stay as they are.

Merge request The cook hands the draft to the head chef, who comments and takes it into the house pages, usually after the line has passed.

Kitchen · check and pack

Pipeline A card in the recipe book sets the stations (cook, check, pack, serve) every draft passes; a failing station normally stops the line.

Runner Kitchen hands do the work at each station; the town’s shopping centre (13) lends some, and any restaurant can hire its own.

Security scan At the check station an inspector reads recipes for risky steps, checks ingredients against recall lists and spots passwords left on cards.

Registry Finished components wait in the cold store, each labelled with its dish and version, until the serve station sends them out.

Dining rooms · serve

Environment Dishes go to named rooms next door, a tasting room and the main hall; the restaurant logs what each room got, and when.

Doors and town · ways in

Web interface At the front door, anyone with a key card sees the board, book, line and rooms, and can act on them by hand.

Git The book follows a common method: each cook keeps a full copy at home and swaps changes through the staff door.

API Through the service hatch, machines and scripts read the board, hand in drafts and start the line.

Doors and town · where the restaurant stands

GitLab.com The restaurant rents a unit in a big shopping centre that its operator runs for all tenants.

Self-managed The owner puts up the building on owned or rented ground and runs it.

Doors and town · three ways to set up the business

GitLab Every restaurant gets office, kitchen and cold store under one roof, one staff list and one set of keys, and sends dishes out.

GitHub The busiest food hall in town, full of cooks; its own stands cover most jobs and a big market of add-on stalls the rest.

Separate tools Specialist shops along a street, a ticket office here, a test kitchen there, each with its own staff list and goods carried between.
