Learning sheet · The Swiss pension system

Three kitchens for your old age

The village street below is a picture of the Swiss pension system, from paying in while you work to your plate in old age. Page two gives the facts behind each numbered item.

The village kitchen
1st pillar · state pension (AHV/IV)

AHV contributions. Everyone who earns fills the village kitchen's basket, a worker with a share of every wage that the employer matches; the village adds a share from tax money, and what comes in this month is served to the old this month.

Contribution years. A stamp card at the kitchen door gets one stamp for every year you paid in, and every missing stamp makes your monthly bowl a little smaller for life.

IV (disability insurance). If illness or an accident stops you working before retirement age, the same kitchen first helps you back to work and, only if that fails, starts filling a bowl for you early.

AHV pension. From retirement age the kitchen fills a bowl for you every month for life, plus an extra one each December: bigger if you brought more, but never more than twice the smallest full bowl, and a married couple together never more than three.

Supplementary benefits (EL). If your bowls and jars together do not cover the basics and you have little put aside, you can ask the town hall next door to top up your bowl from tax money.

The storeroom at work
2nd pillar · occupational pension (BVG)

Pension fund. Once you are employed and earn enough, a shelf in the storeroom at work carries your name; every month you and your employer put jars on it, and the storekeeper adds interest every year.

Buy-in. If your shelf has gaps, you may add jars of your own to the same shelf, and the tax office gives you a discount on them.

Vested benefits. When you change jobs, your shelf moves in a sealed crate to the next employer's storeroom (you tell the old storekeeper where), or waits in a locker until there is one.

Pension or lump sum. At retirement the storekeeper turns your shelf into a fixed monthly delivery for life; if you ask in time, you may take part of it at once, or all of it if the storeroom's rules allow.

Your kitchen at home
3rd pillar · private provision (3a/3b)

Pillar 3a. At home, a locked cupboard takes up to a fixed number of jars each year, the tax office gives you a discount on them, and it opens only near retirement or for a few big steps such as buying a home.

Pillar 3b. Next to it, your open pantry holds whatever else you put aside, with no limit and no lock, and usually no tax discount.
