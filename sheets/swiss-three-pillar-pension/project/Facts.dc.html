AHV contributions

Wage deductions for the 1st pillar, with no upper limit.

 Employee and employer 5.3% each (AHV, IV, EO together); self-employed up to 10%; non-earners 530–26,500 a year. Pay-as-you-go: they fund this year's pensions, with federal money and VAT; a reserve covers about a year. Due from the year you turn 18 if you work, and from the year you turn 21 for everyone living here.
Contribution years

Years recorded in your individual AHV account.

 Every year with at least the minimum paid; a partner's payment covers a non-earning spouse. A full pension needs 44 years (ages 21 to 64); child-raising and care credits raise your average income. Each missing year costs at least 1/44 of the pension for life; you can make up only the last 5 years.
IV (disability insurance)

Insurance for lasting loss of earning ability.

 Paid from the same deductions (1.4% of wages, shared) and federal money; a pension needs 3 years paid. First helps you back to work, such as retraining; a pension from 40% disability, in full from 70%. Apply early. At reference age the AHV pension takes over; no 13th payment.
AHV pension

Monthly pension for life; survivors' pensions too.

 Anyone with one full contribution year; set by your years and average income, split between spouses. From 65 (women 1961–63: 64¼/64½/64¾): 1,260–2,520 a month; a couple at most 3,780; a 13th each December. Register 3–4 months before. Drawing it 1–2 years early cuts it for life; deferring up to 5 years raises it.
Supplementary benefits (EL)

Means-tested top-up to an AHV or IV pension.

 Residents whose income falls short of living costs; assets up to 100,000 (couples 200,000), home excluded. The canton tops up the gap each month and refunds illness costs; only on application. Not paid abroad. Heirs repay the last 10 years' EL from the part of an estate above 40,000.
Pension fund

Pensionskasse: your savings in your employer's fund.

 Employees earning over 22,680 a year from one employer; the employer pays at least half. Saves 7–18% of the wage minus 26,460 (up to 90,720), more with age, from 25; at least 1.25% interest. Part-time work or several small jobs often stay below the threshold. It also covers disability and death.
Buy-in

A voluntary extra payment into your pension fund.

 Up to the gap your fund calculates, for example after a career break or a pay rise. Raises your benefits; the payment is deducted from taxable income. No lump sum from it for 3 years; repay home withdrawals first.
Vested benefits

Your savings when you leave a fund (Freizügigkeit).

 Anyone leaving a job before retirement; you tell the old fund where to send it. Moves to the new employer's fund, or else to a vested-benefits account or policy at a bank or insurer. Unclaimed, a national fund holds it. Cash only to emigrate (EU/EFTA: partly), start self-employment, or for a very small balance.
Pension or lump sum

How the fund pays out at retirement.

 Normally from 65; many funds allow early retirement from 58. A lifelong pension: 6.8% a year on the legal minimum part (100,000 gives 6,800); often less above it. A lump sum (by law a quarter of the legal part, more if the fund allows) needs notice and a spouse's written consent.
Pillar 3a

Tied pension saving with a tax deduction.

 Only with earned income liable to AHV: up to 7,258 a year with a pension fund, else 20% of income up to 36,288. Deducted from taxable income; paid out at the earliest 5 years before reference age, taxed lower. Locked, except for a home, self-employment, emigrating or a full IV pension. Gaps since 2025 can be paid later.
Pillar 3b

Free saving: accounts, funds, insurance, property.

 Anyone, any amount, any time. Available whenever you want; no real tax deduction (premiums only count in the small insurance deduction). Nothing is locked, so it is easy to spend early.

Rules to remember

  • Pillar 1 is compulsory for all, pillar 2 for employees above the threshold; pillar 3 is voluntary.
  • Pillar 1 is pay-as-you-go; pillars 2 and 3 are your own savings, built up in advance.
  • Pillars 1 and 2 together aim at about 60% of your last wage; pillar 3 adds what you want on top.
