How vaccines train the immune system: the facts

Numbers, names, icons and phase markers match page one. Products are examples only.

No. Item What it is What it does Timing
In the town scene (all phases)
Antigen
 The part of a germ (often a protein) that immune cells recognise. Lets immune cells pick out this one germ; every vaccine brings it or its recipe. In every dose.
Innate immune cells
 Fast, general defenders such as dendritic cells; no memory. Start inflammation; dendritic cells carry the antigen to the nearest lymph node. Within hours of the shot.
Side effects
 Sore arm, tiredness, headache, fever, sometimes swollen lymph nodes. Pass on their own; severe allergy is rare, about 1 in a million doses. Days 1⁠–⁠3 (nodes: weeks); after MMR, fever or rash on days 7⁠–⁠12.
Helper T cell
 A CD4 T cell in the lymph node that recognises the antigen. Activates B cells and killer T cells; needed for strong antibodies and memory. Activated within days.
B cell
 A white blood cell whose surface receptor fits one antigen. Becomes plasma cells that release antibodies; some become memory B cells. Antibodies appear after about a week.
Antibody
 A Y-shaped protein from plasma cells that fits one antigen. Binds the germ or its toxin, stops it entering cells and marks it for destruction. Peak about 4 weeks after the shot, then fall over months.
Killer T cell
 A CD8 T cell that spots antigen pieces on infected cells. Makes infected cells self-destruct, so a virus cannot multiply in them. Peak at about 2 weeks; stronger with live and mRNA vaccines than with protein ones.
Memory cells
 Long-lived memory B and T cells; some plasma cells keep making antibody. When the real germ comes they respond in days, not weeks, often before illness. Years to decades; for measles lifelong.
Booster
 A later dose of the same or an updated vaccine. Raises antibody levels, makes antibodies fit tighter and can match a changed germ. Months to years later; flu yearly, tetanus every 10⁠–⁠20 years.
The kit: kinds of vaccine and the add-on
Whole-germ vaccine
 The whole germ, live but weakened (MMR) or inactivated (polio IPV). Shows many antigens; live ones multiply a little, so not in pregnancy or with weak immunity. Live: 1⁠–⁠2 doses often last; inactivated: boosters.
mRNA vaccine
 A recipe (mRNA) for one antigen in tiny fat droplets (e.g. Comirnaty, Spikevax). Cells briefly make the antigen; the mRNA is broken down and never enters the nucleus. 1⁠–⁠2 doses; updated versions follow new variants.
Protein vaccine
 Purified germ pieces (hepatitis B, HPV); toxoids (tetanus) hold a disarmed toxin. Shows only the chosen antigen and cannot cause infection; often paired with an adjuvant. Often 2⁠–⁠3 doses; some need boosters (tetanus).
Adjuvant
 An additive, e.g. aluminium salts, in many non-live vaccines. Alerts innate immune cells for a stronger, longer-lasting response. First days, at the injection site; adds to soreness.
The real gang: the community
Herd immunity
 Indirect protection of a population when enough people are immune. Shelters those who cannot be vaccinated, e.g. babies; only for germs spread between people. Share needed: measles about 95%, polio about 80%.

Rules to remember

  1. A vaccine shows the antigen, not the illness; the immune system does the learning.
  2. Protection builds over weeks, some vaccines need several doses, and no vaccine protects 100%.
  3. Antibodies fade, memory lasts years; a booster tops both up and can match a changed germ.
  4. Mild side effects in the days after a dose are normal signs of the reaction; having none does not mean the vaccine failed.
  5. The more people are immune, the less a germ can reach those who cannot be vaccinated.

Questions people ask

Can a vaccine give me the disease? Not if it holds no live germ; a live vaccine can cause a mild form, very rarely a serious one.

Does mRNA change my DNA? No: it never enters the cell nucleus and is soon broken down.

Why is my armpit swollen? The nearby lymph node is building the immune response.

Why a flu shot every year? Flu viruses change their antigens, and protection fades within months.

Why not just catch the disease? It also brings immunity, but with the risk of serious illness.
