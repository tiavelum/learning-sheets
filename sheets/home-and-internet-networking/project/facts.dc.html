In the picture
Device
Any computer, phone, TV or gadget on the network.Turns what you do into packets, and back.A network adapter and an IP address.
Ethernet cable
Copper cable, RJ45 plugs, up to 100 m long.Carries electric signals; steady, often 1000 Mbit/s.A free socket on the router or on a switch.
Wi-Fi
Local radio network on 2.4, 5 or 6 GHz.Links devices by radio; all share its speed.Its name and password (WPA2/WPA3 codes the link).
Home router
A small router plus extras, often with Wi-Fi and modem.Hands out private addresses, shares the public one (NAT), filters traffic.Power and an access line to the provider.
Packet
A small block of data, often up to 1,500 bytes.Carries a message in pieces, each routed alone.A header with sender and recipient addresses.
IP address
A device's number: IPv4 like 192.0.2.10, or longer IPv6.Lets routers send packets to it, and replies back.To be unique: private at home (e.g. 192.168.1.20), public from the ISP.
Encryption (TLS)
Transport Layer Security: the "s" in https.Codes the content; proves the site's name.A certificate signed by a trusted authority.
Access line
Fibre (light), DSL (phone wire) or TV cable.Carries your home's traffic; sets its top speed.A modem (ONT for fibre), often in the router.
Mobile network
Cell towers of a mobile operator: 4G, 5G.Keeps you online on the move, tower to tower.A SIM or eSIM with a plan, and a tower in range.
Internet service provider
ISP: the company that connects you to the internet.Gives you a public IP address and links you to other networks.Links to other networks, which pass traffic on.
DNS
Domain Name System: the internet's directory.Turns example.com into an IP address; answers are cached for a set time.A resolver: usually the ISP's, or a public one such as 9.9.9.9.
Routers
Junctions of networks; a path crosses many.Pass each packet one hop nearer its goal.Routes learned from neighbours (e.g. BGP).
Backbone
High-capacity fibre-optic lines between cities and continents.Carries data as light; sea cables carry over 99 % of intercontinental traffic.Repeaters to boost the light, and ships to repair breaks.
Server
A computer that answers requests.Serves web, mail, files; each on a port number.Power, cooling and a public address, nonstop.
On the card: two ways of delivering
TCP
Transmission Control Protocol: most web, e-mail, files.Opens a connection, numbers data, confirms, resends, reorders.Only the two ends confirm and resend; routers do not.
UDP
User Datagram Protocol: calls, games, DNS.Sends at once: low delay, losses stay lost.No setup; the program handles gaps itself.
Remember
  1. Everything travels as packets, routed one by one; TCP puts them back together.
  2. Names are for people, numbers for machines: DNS translates first.
  3. Speed has two sides: bandwidth (Mbit/s, how much at once) and latency (ms, how long a packet takes).
  4. The padlock means coded and the name checked, not that the site is honest.
  5. "The cloud" is servers in data centres that someone else runs.
