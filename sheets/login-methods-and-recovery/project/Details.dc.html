Item What it is Enough on its own? Where to keep it
Unlocking and storing your logins
1Passcode, face or fingerprint
 Unlocks your device; checked on it, not sent to services. Yes, with the device in hand. Passcode in your head; type it out of sight.
2Phone or computer
 Runs your password manager; holds your passkeys. Yes for saved logins; 2FA still applies, except with passkeys. With you; if lost, remove its passkeys.
3Password manager
 Encrypted app for passwords and passkeys, often synced. No: it needs your device unlock (1) or its own password. On your devices; never its own recovery items.
4Password manager account
 Account holding an encrypted copy of your password manager. Usually yes: signing in opens it. If only you hold the key, your password or device screen lock opens it. Password in your head; set up its recovery early.
Logging in to services
5Password
 Secret you type; the service stores it hashed (scrambled). Yes, unless 2FA is on. Password manager; 15+ random, unique characters.
6Two-step login (2FA)
 Second check after the password: app code, text or prompt. No: only together with the password. Prefer app codes to texts; approve a prompt only if you started the login.
7Passkey
 Digital key pair per site; the service holds only the half that can’t log in. Yes, with your device and its unlock (1). Synced in a password manager, or on one device.
Other ways to log in
8Email login link
 One-time link or code mailed instead of a password. Yes, for whoever reads your mailbox. Guard the mailbox: passkey, or password + 2FA.
9Sign in with another account
 Login via an account at e.g. Google, Apple or Microsoft. Yes, for whoever controls that account. Guard that account; review its linked apps.
Getting back in
10Recovery email or phone
 Address or number where the service sends a reset link or code. Often: whoever controls it may reset your password. Ones you control; keep them current.
11Recovery phrase or key
 Word list or code that resets your password and unlocks data only you can read. Sometimes alone (Proton); Apple also needs your trusted phone number. On paper; never only inside what it recovers.
12Recovery contact
 Trusted person who gives you a code or confirms a number. No: they cannot enter your account. Someone reliable and reachable.
13Identity check
 Provider checks that you own the account, e.g. by questions. No: the provider decides, sometimes after days. Last resort; keep recovery details current.
142FA recovery codes
 Single-use codes that replace a lost second check. Usually no: the password is still needed. On paper, locked away, or in the password manager, except its own.

Two questions for any service

Q1

How do I log in here? Password, passkey, email link or another account: that is what to guard.

Q2

If I lose everything, can the provider let me back in? For end-to-end encrypted data (only you can read it), often not: only your recovery phrase or key, or on some services a recovery contact, can.

Three rules

R1

Use a passkey where offered, otherwise a long, unique password with 2FA. Take most care with what opens other logins: your password manager account (4), your mailbox (8, 10) and the account you sign in with (9).

R2

Recovery methods can also let someone else in: keep only those you need, and keep them current.

R3

Never store a recovery item only inside what it recovers; keep paper ones locked away.
