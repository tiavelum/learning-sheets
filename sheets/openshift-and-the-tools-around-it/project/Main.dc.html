OpenShift as a container market

OpenShift is Red Hat’s platform for running many apps in containers on many machines. Picture it as this market: every number marks one real part or alternative, and page two gives the facts behind it.

Market office(control plane)
Market grounds(where apps run)
Service yard(supply, security, tools)
Other markets

API server The office counter: every order is handed in here and kept in the ledger.

Scheduler The plot planner, who picks a plot with room for each new shop.

Controller A warden who checks the grounds against the ledger and fixes any gap.

Operator A warden with expert skills for one facility, like the cold store.

Node A plot with room and its own power hook-up; it holds several shops.

Pod An open shop: one container, or a few bolted together, on one plot.

Deployment The standing order “keep three soup bars open at all times”.

Service A fixed shop sign inside the market that leads to any identical shop.

Route The signpost at the gate that sends street visitors to a shop sign.

Project One tenant’s lease, with its own keys; its shops fly its pennant.

Container image A sealed template box in the depot; every shop opens from a copy.

Build and pipeline The workshop line that packs, tests and ships a tenant’s new box.

Access control (RBAC, SCC) Badge office for people; house rules for shops: hands off the wiring.

Web console and oc The service desk: map, meters and a terminal; orders go on to the counter.
