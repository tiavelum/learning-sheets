The scene (page 1, picture): items from all four places
AHV/IV contributions
Compulsory levy on all earned income; pay-as-you-go.10.6 % of salary, half from employer. Non-earners pay too.No pot in your name; each year counts for your pension.
Contribution years
Years with contributions on your AHV account (IK).44 years, age 21 to 65; gaps payable only 5 years back.Each missing year: 1/44 (2.3 %) less pension for life.
AHV pension
Lifelong old-age pension; survivors' pensions are separate.From 65, or 63 (less) to 70 (more); set by years (2) and income.CHF 1,260–2,520 a month (couples max. 3,780), 13 times a year.
IV pension
Disability pension; rehabilitation comes first.From 40 % disability, after a year; min. 3 contribution years.40 % gives a quarter pension, 70 % a full one; AHV at 65.
Supplementary benefits (EL)
Means-tested top-up to minimum living costs; a legal right.On application; assets under CHF 100,000 (single), own home excluded.Paid from taxes, not contributions; tax-free.
Pension fund savings
Capital in your name at your employer's pension fund.Compulsory above CHF 22,680 salary; employer pays ≥ half.Mandatory part earns at least 1.25 %; also covers disability and death.
Buy-in
Voluntary payment to fill a gap in your savings (6).Up to your fund's limit; then no lump sum for 3 years.Deductible from taxable income.
Pillar 3a
Tied pension account or policy, only with earned income.Max. CHF 7,258 a year (no pension fund: 20 % of income, max. 36,288).Tax-deductible; opens 5 years before reference age (earlier for home, business, emigration).
Pillar 3b
All other saving: accounts, funds, property, insurance.No limits, no conditions.Free access; usually no tax deduction.
Pension gap
What you need minus what pillars 1 and 2 pay.Pillars 1 and 2 aim at about 60 % of last salary.Bigger with high or part-time pay or gaps; filled by 7, 11, 12.
Tank at work: changing jobs
Vested benefits
Your savings (6) when you leave a pension fund.To the new fund; between jobs to a vested benefits account.Cash mainly if you emigrate outside the EU/EFTA or go self-employed.
Tank at work: at retirement, tap or carry away
Pension-fund pension
Lifelong monthly pension from your savings.Savings × conversion rate: min. 6.8 % at 65 (mandatory part).CHF 100,000 → CHF 6,800 a year; spouse usually gets 60 % after you.
Lump sum
Savings paid out at once, instead of or with a pension.A quarter by law, often all; spouse must agree in writing.You carry market and long-life risk; taxed once, at a reduced rate.

Remember

  1. 1st pillar: basic needs. 1st + 2nd: about 60 % of your last salary. 3rd: your own extra.
  2. The 1st passes money on, from workers to retirees; the 2nd and 3rd save in your name.
  3. Reference age 65 (women born 1962 or 1963: a few months less). Register for the AHV pension 3 to 4 months ahead.

Ask yourself

  1. Does my IK extract show every year since I turned 21?
  2. What does my pension certificate project at 65?
  3. Pension or lump sum: who should carry the risk of a long life, my fund or me?

From 1 January 2027: AHV CHF 1,280–2,560 a month, pension fund compulsory above CHF 23,040, pillar 3a up to CHF 7,373. Sources: ahv-iv.ch, bsv.admin.ch, ch.ch. A learning aid, not financial advice.
