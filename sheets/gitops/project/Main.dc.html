GitOps and its tools

This sheet pictures GitOps as a supermarket chain whose stores keep their shelves exactly as head office plans them. Pages 1–2 tell the scene; pages 3–5 give the facts behind each number.

Places in the scene: Head office Factory & warehouse Store Building

The scene Each number below is marked in the picture.

Git repository

The archive binder at head office. It holds every shelf plan, and each past edition stays filed with its date and author.

Pull request

A change request: someone proposes an edit to a plan, a colleague checks and signs it, and only then is it filed in the binder.

Manifest

One plan sheet. It says what belongs on a shelf and how many, never how to put it there.

CI pipeline

The factory line. It turns a new product design into a finished, tested product and packs it; it never drives to a store.

Container image

A sealed carton with the product and all it needs. Its label names the version; only its seal number pins the exact carton.

Container registry

The warehouse where cartons wait, sorted by label, until a store sends for them.

Update bot

A clerk who spots new carton versions in the warehouse and writes them into the plan, directly or as a change request.

Kubernetes cluster

The store. Its floor staff keep every shelf stocked to whatever sheet is pinned on the store's notice board.

Policy engine

A warden at the notice board who refuses any sheet that breaks the chain's rules, whoever brings it.

Read the picture: people change plans at head office through signed change requests; new products start at the factory and wait in the warehouse until the store fetches them. Page 2 shows how the plan reaches the store.
