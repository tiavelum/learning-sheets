Points of sail
The wind hill
Learning sheet · page 1 of 2

Picture the wind as a hill: it blows down from the summit, and your boat is a hiker on the slope. Each numbered path is one way to sail; page two gives the sailing facts behind each number.

Zones Climbing towards the summit Walking across or down Turning round
On the hill

The no-go zone is the wedge of directions pointing straight up at the summit: too steep to climb, so a path aimed into it stops you, and you slide back.

Close-hauled is the steepest path you can still climb, just beside the wedge: hard, slow work, but it gains the most height.

A close reach is a gentler path that still climbs, between the steepest path and the level one: easier and quicker going.

A beam reach is the level path along the slope, the summit straight to your side: you neither climb nor descend, and you stride out fast.

A broad reach slants down the slope, the summit behind your shoulder: quick, easy going.

Running is the path straight down, the summit right behind you: you take short, wobbly steps, and one slip can swing you round (see 8).

Turning round
Face to the summit

Tacking is the hairpin bend of a zig-zag climb: you turn with your face to the summit, cross the wedge in one smooth, steady turn, and climb on in the other direction.

Back to the summit

Gybing is the bend of a zig-zag descent: you turn with your back to the summit. Taken on purpose, after a call, it is smooth; a slip that turns you by accident swings you round hard.
