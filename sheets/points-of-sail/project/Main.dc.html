Points of sail

Picture the wind as a hillside: it comes down from the summit, so sailing toward the wind is like climbing and sailing away from it is like walking downhill. Page two gives the facts behind each numbered item.

Up the slope (upwind)
Across the slope, climbing or dropping, or level (reaching)
Down the slope (downwind)
A turn takes the zone of its type: a tack is up the slope, a gybe is down the slope.
The no-go zone is the wedge straight up toward the summit: no path climbs it, and whoever heads into it stops and slides back.
Close-hauled is the steepest path you can still climb, angled as close to the summit as any path goes.
Tacking is the corner of the zig-zag on the way up: your face turns through the direction of the summit, which then lies on your other side.
A close reach is a path that crosses the slope while still climbing gently.
A beam reach is the level path straight across the slope, with the summit square to your side.
A broad reach is a path that crosses the slope while dropping, with the summit behind your shoulder.
Running is the path straight down the slope, with the summit right at your back.
Gybing is the corner of the zig-zag on the way down: your back turns through the direction of the summit, and this corner is taken smoothly and under control.

The hut in the middle is your boat; each path is a course you could steer from it. Unlike a hiker, a boat goes fastest across the slope, not straight down it.
