How a web page reaches your screen

Page 1 of 2: the scene

This page is a picture of the topic: one errand, ordering a flat-pack shelf from a distant workshop and having it built at home. Page two gives the facts behind each numbered item.

Zones Start and look up, 1–5 Connect and lock, 6–9 Ask and receive, 10–12 Build the page, 13–16

The scene: from your note to the delivery, 1–12

Browser Your helper at home: takes your note, goes out on every errand and brings back the parts.
Web address (URL) Your note: “send securely”, the workshop’s name, and the piece you want.
DNS resolver The enquiry desk your helper asks for the workshop’s street address; it remembers recent answers.
Name servers The directories the desk consults in turn: the main index, the index for the name’s ending, then the workshop’s own register.
IP address The workshop’s street address, written in numbers, which goes on every parcel.
Routers The sorting stations along the roads, from the one by your door; each passes every note and parcel one station nearer its street address.
TCP connection The delivery agreement: three short notes first (“ready?”, “ready, you?”, “ready”), then every parcel is numbered, signed for, and resent if lost.
TLS encryption The lockboxes: helper and workshop agree on a combination without ever saying it aloud; from then on every parcel travels locked.
Certificate The workshop’s ID card, stamped by an issuing office your helper trusts; it arrives locked and is checked before any order goes out.
HTTP request The order form, sent in a locked box: it asks the workshop to send the piece named on your note.
Web server The workshop clerk, who reads each order form and packs what it asks for.
HTTP response The delivery to your door, with a slip on top saying “here it is” or “no such piece”.

Build the page, 13–16: what the browser builds it from

HTMLThe assembly plan in the delivery: which boards, in what order, what is written on them, and which further boxes to order.
CSSThe finishing guide, usually a further box: colours, sizes and wishes for where boards sit; your helper works out the exact spots.
JavaScriptThe small motor, from a further box or built into the plan: it changes parts when you touch them, even after the shelf stands.
RenderingYour helper follows plan and finishing guide: assembles the boards, measures, paints, and sets the finished shelf in your living room.
