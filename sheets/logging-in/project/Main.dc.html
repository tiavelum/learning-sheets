Logging in

First: unlock your phone Then: open the door Then: pass the doorman If something is lost: get back in

Picture each online account as your own flat in its own building; its door, doorman and office all belong to that building. Page 2 gives the facts behind each numbered item.

  1. Device passcode The code that unlocks your phone. It stays on the phone, and almost nothing inside can be used until it is entered.

  2. Biometrics Your fingerprint or face unlocks the phone instead of the code; the phone checks it itself, shows it to no door, and falls back on the code.

  3. Password manager A notebook with a different long word for every door; your phone's unlock or one master word opens it, and it offers each word only at its own door.

  4. Password A secret word you type at the door's keypad; whoever catches it at a fake door or cracks it from a stolen list can open every door that uses it.

  5. Passkey A key made for this one door, used with your phone's unlock; the building keeps only the matching lock, and the phone offers the key at no other door.

Then: pass the doorman

Not every building has one; this second check is called two-step login.

SMS code

The doorman texts a one-time number to your phone; whoever moves your number to their phone gets it too.

Authenticator app

An app shows a number that changes about every half minute; the doorman works out the same one; no message needed.

Sign-in prompt

The doorman asks your phone: is this you at the door? Tap Yes only if you are there; No if it keeps asking.

Security key

A fob on your keyring you touch to the doorman's reader; like a passkey, it answers only the real building.

If something is lost: get back in

Arrange 10 to 12 before anything is lost.

Recovery email and phone

A spare mailbox and number given to the office in advance; it sends a way back in when you forget your word.

Recovery codes

Printed one-time passes from the building, kept at home; each gets you past the doorman once without your phone.

Second device

A second phone or computer the building already knows; the doorman accepts it when your first phone is lost.

Account recovery

With nothing else left, you ask the office to let you in; it checks you, may make you wait days, and can refuse.
