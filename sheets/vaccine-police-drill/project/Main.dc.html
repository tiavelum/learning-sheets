This page pictures vaccination as a police drill in a town: the town is one person, its police the immune system and a gang a germ. Page two gives the facts behind each numbered item.

The town and its police: the drill, step by step

Antigen. The gang's emblem: the mark by which the police will know every member of the gang.

Innate immune cells. The neighbourhood watch at the gate: they notice trouble, like the flashing light, sound the alarm, and some carry the emblem to the station.

Side effects. The noise of the drill: the watch's sirens, roadblocks at the gate and a tired town for a few days; serious trouble is very rare.

Helper T cell. The police chief at the station: checks the emblem and gives the go-ahead to the workshop and the squad.

B cell. The workshop at the station: usually once the chief agrees, it starts making clamps that fit only this emblem.

Antibody. The Y-shaped clamps sent out into the streets: they grip the emblem so the gang member cannot get into a house and gets picked up.

Killer T cell. The special squad: it learns the emblem and shuts down, one at a time, the houses the gang has taken over.

Memory cells. The veterans: officers and workshop masters who stay on after the drill and act far faster than the first time when the real gang comes.

Booster. A refresher drill months or years later: the veterans train again and turn out more clamps that fit better.

The kit: what each kind of vaccine brings

Three common kinds of kit (others exist): each vaccine uses one
Add-on to the patch (4)
Whole-germ vaccine

A whole gang member in the kit, made unfit to cause the disease: either weakened so he can barely make trouble, or killed outright.

mRNA vaccine

A sewing pattern for the emblem: houses near the gate sew and show the emblem, then tear the pattern up; it never touches their own blueprints.

Protein vaccine

Just a piece of the gang, with no gang member attached: a ready-made patch that carries the emblem.

Adjuvant

A flashing light clipped to the patch, so the neighbourhood watch cannot overlook it.

The real gang: why enough trained towns matter

Herd immunity. When enough towns in the region have trained police, the gang finds too few open towns to spread through, so the towns that could not train are much less likely to be reached.
