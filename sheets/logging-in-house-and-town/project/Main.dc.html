LoginsHow you prove it is you, and how you get back in

This page is a picture of logins: your phone or computer is your house, and each online account is a building in town. Page two gives the facts behind each numbered item.

At home: your house and its cloud copy
At the door: of a building in town
If lost: ways to get back in

The scene

Device passcode: The code of the keypad lock on your own front door: it opens your house, and no building in town ever sees it.
Password reset: When you forget the word, the building posts a fresh-start link to your mailbox, your email; whoever opens it can do the same.
Biometrics: The front door also knows your face or fingertip and opens without the code; after a restart it asks for the code again.
Recovery codes: A sheet of spare one-time tickets the building gives you with the second proof, kept in your garden shed; each replaces it once.
Password manager: A key cabinet in your house that keeps the word and the key for each building, and offers them only at the matching door.
Recovery contact: A friend you named in advance who can give you a code from their own house when you are locked out.
Master password: The one combination that opens the key cabinet, so you learn it by heart; some cabinets open with the door code instead.
Recovery key: A long spare key you chose to seal in your garden shed; the building keeps no spare, so lose it too and the door stays shut.
Sync: A locked copy of your key cabinet in a storehouse in the clouds, so a new house gets the same words and keys.
Account recovery: The building's front desk, which checks who you are by its own rules and may make you wait days when nothing else works.

At the door: one or the other

Passkey

A key from your cabinet that fits one building's lock; the building keeps only the lock, so a fake door gets nothing.

Password

A secret word you say at a building's door; anyone who overhears it, or a fake door that asks for it, can use it too.

At the door: a second proof

Two-step login

After the word, the door asks for a second proof that only you carry, so an overheard word alone no longer opens it.

Text-message code

The building texts a one-time number to your phone number, and you read it out at the door.

Authenticator app

A small clock in your house shows a changing number; the building works out the same number at its door.

Security key

A small fob you carry on your keyring and plug in or touch at the door; it answers only the real door.
