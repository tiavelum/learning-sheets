DevOps, pictured as a restaurant

Page 1 of 2 · the picture

This page pictures DevOps as a restaurant: the recipes are the code, the dishes on the tables are the running software, and the guests are its users. Page two gives the facts behind each numbered item.

Development (Dev) The cooks who invent new dishes and change existing ones; they want something new on the menu often.
Version control The shared recipe book that records every change with who made it, when and why, so any earlier version can be cooked again.
Automated tests The thermometer and scale that check every batch against the recipe card, the same way every time, without anyone having to remember.
Infrastructure as Code (IaC) The written station plan (equipment, settings, what goes where); a machine sets up an identical station from it, anywhere, the same every time.
Continuous delivery (CD) Every dish that passes the checks takes the same route from stove to table, so it is always ready to go out, and a new dish can go out on any day as a routine, not as an event.
Flow Plates move steadily one way, from kitchen to table, and each waits as little as possible.
Operations (Ops) The waiters and floor crew who keep every evening running (tables, equipment, supplies); they want a calm, predictable service.
Monitoring The screen that shows, during service, how long each table has waited and how full the room is, and rings a bell when something goes wrong.
Feedback What guests say, and what they leave on their plates, reaches the cooks the same evening, not weeks later.
DORA metrics The numbers on the whiteboard, such as how often new dishes go out, how long from a finished change to the table, how many come back and how fast a bad night is put right.
Kitchen
Continuous integration (CI)
Every cook adds small changes to the shared recipe at least once a day, and each change is cooked and checked at once by the thermometer and scale, so clashes show while they are small.
Pass: before and after
Silo
Before: a closed door between kitchen and dining room; cooks push new dishes through, waiters take the complaints, and each crew blames the other.
DevOps
After: kitchen and dining-room staff work as one crew across an open pass, answerable together for each dish from recipe to the guest's last bite; every other item here is a way this crew works.
Pass
Small batches
One new dish at a time, instead of a whole new menu on one big night.
Staff table
Blameless postmortem
After a bad night the crew asks what in the setup let it happen, never who is to blame.
Continual learning
The crew sets time aside, tries one small change to how it works, and keeps what helps.
